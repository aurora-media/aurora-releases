What you get

A library that looks like it cost a subscription.

Point Aurora at your folders. It reads the filenames, fetches the artwork and gets out of the way.

Films, shows, anime, docs

Seasons and episodes are worked out from your folder names, including the awkward anime numbering everything else gets wrong.

Artwork that just appears

Posters, backdrops, cast and episode stills from TMDB, AniList and TheTVDB. Wrong match? Fix it once and lock it so re-scans never undo your work.

Flexible playback, on your devices

Direct play when your device can handle the file, on-demand transcoding when it can't. Resume on the TV exactly where the laptop left off.

Every screen you own

Installers are being prepared for Windows, macOS and Linux, with browser access on other devices — including your television's, which drives the whole thing with the remote you already have: arrows, OK, back. No box to buy, nothing to sideload. A phone and tablet app is on its way.

Everyone in the house

Separate accounts with their own watch history, lists and resume points. Unlimited, at no extra cost.

Nothing phones home

No telemetry, no account with us, no licence server. Aurora works with the internet unplugged, and your library is nobody's business but yours.

Questions

The things people ask first.

Does Aurora come with films or shows?

No — and it never will. Aurora is an empty player: you point it at video files you already own and it organises them. The only content it offers is the Classic cinema catalogue, which is public-domain films hosted by the Internet Archive. Aurora links to them; it doesn't host or distribute anything.

Where does my media live? Do you see any of it?

On your own machine, and no. There is no cloud component. We never see your files, your library, or even that you're running Aurora. It keeps working with the network unplugged.

What hardware do I need?

Aurora runs on a Raspberry Pi, NAS, mini PC or desktop. Playback performance depends on the file and browser: direct play and remux are lightweight, while software transcoding of large HEVC files may be too demanding for a Raspberry Pi. Public builds for Linux, Windows and macOS are being prepared.

Is it really free? What's the catch?

Yes, all of it, and there isn't one. There is no paid tier above the free one and nothing is held back. If you'd like to support the work there is a donation link, but it is optional and nothing unlocks — everything is already yours.

Can I move it to another machine?

Yes. Nothing is tied to hardware. Move the data folder across and carry on.

Help

Something broken? Say so.

Aurora is built by one person, so a problem you report is read by the person who can fix it. There is no ticket queue and no support tier.

Report a bug

The version from Dashboard → Updates, what you did and what happened instead is enough to start with. Issues are public, so anyone hitting the same thing can see it is known.
